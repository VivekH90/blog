Energy Transmission in One-Dimensional Strings

Energy conservation, travelling waves, Fourier spectra, and transmission at a junction between strings.

§11. Introduction

The aim of this article is to understand the basic ideas of energy propagation and transmission in one-dimensional strings. We would also explore the idea of impedance, which essentially captures how a medium responds to a travelling disturbance.

§22. Energy and Related Concepts

§2.12.1. The Lagrangian and Hamiltonian Description

For a linear string with mass density \(\mu\) and tension \(T\), the Lagrangian density is given by

\[\mathcal{L} =\frac12\mu\left(\frac{\partial y}{\partial t}\right)^2 -\frac12T\left(\frac{\partial y}{\partial x}\right)^2.\]

From this, we obtain the conjugate momentum density:

\[\pi(x,t)=\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial y_t} =\mu y_t.\]

The Hamiltonian density is therefore

\[\mathcal{H}=\pi y_t-\mathcal{L},\]

so that

\[\mathcal{H} =\frac{\pi^2}{2\mu}+\frac{T}{2}y_x^2 =\frac12\mu y_t^2+\frac12T y_x^2.\]

§2.22.2. Power and Local Energy Conservation: The Discrete Picture

At first, one might think that the energy within every small segment of a string must be conserved, so that no energy is transferred into or out of it.

This, however, is not the case.

To see why, consider a discrete model of the string.

The force on the \(n\)-th particle, to first order, is given by

\[F_n=\frac{T}{a}(y_{n+1}-y_n) -\frac{T}{a}(y_n-y_{n-1}),\]

where \(a\) is the separation between adjacent masses.

Define

\[q_n=y_{n+1}-y_n,\qquad q_{n-1}=y_n-y_{n-1}.\]

The kinetic energy of the \(n\)-th particle is

\[K_n=\frac12m\dot y_n^2.\]

Hence,

\[\frac{dK_n}{dt} =m\dot y_n\ddot y_n =\frac{T}{a}\dot y_n(q_n-q_{n-1}).\]

Likewise, the potential energy can be written as

\[U_n=\frac{T}{4a}(q_{n-1}^2+q_n^2).\]

Differentiating, we obtain

\[\frac{dU_n}{dt} =\frac{T}{2a} \left(q_{n-1}\dot q_{n-1}+q_n\dot q_n\right).\]

The total energy associated with the \(n\)-th particle is \(E_n=K_n+U_n\). Therefore,

\[\begin{aligned} \frac{dE_n}{dt} &=\frac{dK_n}{dt}+\frac{dU_n}{dt}\\ &=\frac{T}{2a}\big[ q_n(\dot y_{n+1}+\dot y_n)\\ &\qquad-q_{n-1}(\dot y_n+\dot y_{n-1}) \big]. \end{aligned}\]

In general, this is not zero:

\[\frac{dE_n}{dt}\neq0.\]

However, we can define the energy fluxes across the adjacent links as

\[P_{n+\frac12} =-\frac{T}{2a}q_n(\dot y_{n+1}+\dot y_n),\]

and

\[P_{n-\frac12} =-\frac{T}{2a}q_{n-1}(\dot y_n+\dot y_{n-1}).\]

Our energy equation becomes

\[\frac{dE_n}{dt} =P_{n-\frac12}-P_{n+\frac12}.\]

For an isolated string, we see that

\[\sum_n\frac{dE_n}{dt}=0.\]

Hence, the total energy is conserved.

§2.32.3. The Continuum Limit

Let

\[x=na,\qquad y_n\longrightarrow y,\qquad a\longrightarrow0.\]

Then,

\[y_{n+1}-y_n\sim a\frac{\partial y}{\partial x},\]

and

\[\dot y_{n+1}+\dot y_n \sim2\frac{\partial y}{\partial t}.\]

Thus,

\[\begin{aligned} P_{n+\frac12} &\sim-\frac{T}{2a} \left(a\frac{\partial y}{\partial x}\right) \left(2\frac{\partial y}{\partial t}\right). \end{aligned}\]

Consequently, the energy flux becomes

\[P(x,t)=-T\frac{\partial y}{\partial x} \frac{\partial y}{\partial t}.\]

The discrete energy conservation equation,

\[\frac{dE_n}{dt} =P_{n-\frac12}-P_{n+\frac12},\]

becomes the local energy conservation equation

\[\frac{\partial u}{\partial t} +\frac{\partial P}{\partial x}=0,\]

where \(u(x,t)\) is the energy per unit length.

§2.42.4. A Single Travelling Wave

For a right-moving sinusoidal wave,

\[y(x,t)=A\cos(kx-\omega t), \qquad \omega=vk,\]

we have

\[P(x,t)=TA^2k\omega\sin^2(kx-\omega t).\]

Taking the time average gives

\[\begin{aligned} \langle P\rangle &=\frac12TA^2k\omega\\ &=\frac12\mu v\omega^2A^2, \end{aligned}\]

where \(v=\sqrt{T/\mu}\).

§2.52.5. Superposition and the Fourier Spectrum

For two waves,

\[y=A_1\cos\theta_1+A_2\cos\theta_2,\]

where

\[\theta_j=k_jx-\omega_jt,\]

we have

\[\begin{aligned} P={}&TA_1^2k_1\omega_1\sin^2\theta_1\\ &+TA_2^2k_2\omega_2\sin^2\theta_2\\ &+TA_1A_2(k_1\omega_2+k_2\omega_1) \sin\theta_1\sin\theta_2. \end{aligned}\]

For distinct frequencies, the time average of the cross term vanishes. Hence,

\[\langle P\rangle =\frac12TA_1^2k_1\omega_1 +\frac12TA_2^2k_2\omega_2.\]

In general, for discrete Fourier modes,

\[y=\sum_j A_j\cos\theta_j,\]

the average power is

\[\langle P\rangle =\frac12\sum_j TA_j^2k_j\omega_j,\]

assuming distinct frequencies so that the cross terms average to zero.

§2.62.6. A Complete Fourier Spectrum

A complete Fourier spectrum is represented by

\[y(x,t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}} \int_{-\infty}^{+\infty} A(k)e^{ik(x-vt)}\,dk.\]

For a right-moving profile,

\[y_t=-vy_x.\]

The instantaneous power is therefore

\[P(x,t)=Tv[y_x(x,t)]^2.\]

Using the Fourier representation,

\[P(x,t)=\frac{Tv}{2\pi} \left| \int_{-\infty}^{+\infty} ikA(k)e^{ik(x-vt)}\,dk \right|^2.\]

Parseval's theorem then gives the total energy:

\[E=T\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty} k^2|A(k)|^2\,dk.\]

§33. A Discontinuous Medium

Consider two strings joined at \(x=0\).

The left string has mass density \(\mu_L\), and the right string has mass density \(\mu_R\). The tension must be the same in both strings because the junction does not move horizontally.

The boundary conditions are

\[y_L(0,t)=y_R(0,t),\]

and

\[\frac{\partial y_L(0,t)}{\partial x} =\frac{\partial y_R(0,t)}{\partial x}.\]

For a wave incident from the left,

\[y_L=A_I\cos(k_Lx-\omega t) +A_R\cos(k_Lx+\omega t), \qquad x<0,\]

and

\[y_R=A_T\cos(k_Rx-\omega t), \qquad x>0.\]

Applying the boundary conditions, we obtain

\[A_I+A_R=A_T,\]

and

\[k_L(A_I-A_R)=k_RA_T.\]

Defining the reflection and transmission coefficients by

\[r=\frac{A_R}{A_I}, \qquad t=\frac{A_T}{A_I},\]

we find

\[r=\frac{k_L-k_R}{k_L+k_R} =\frac{\sqrt{\mu_L}-\sqrt{\mu_R}} {\sqrt{\mu_L}+\sqrt{\mu_R}},\]

and

\[t=\frac{2k_L}{k_L+k_R} =\frac{2\sqrt{\mu_L}} {\sqrt{\mu_L}+\sqrt{\mu_R}}.\]

Here, we have used \(k=\omega\sqrt{\mu/T}\), with equal tension and equal angular frequency in both strings.

§3.13.1. Transmission of Energy and Power

Define

\[\phi_I=k_Lx-\omega t,\]
\[\phi_R=k_Lx+\omega t,\]

and

\[\phi_T=k_Rx-\omega t.\]

The energy density in the left string is

\[u_L=\mu_L\omega^2 \left( A_I^2\sin^2\phi_I+ A_R^2\sin^2\phi_R \right),\]

so that

\[\langle u_L\rangle =\frac12\mu_L\omega^2(A_I^2+A_R^2).\]

In the right string,

\[u_R=\mu_R\omega^2A_T^2\sin^2\phi_T,\]

and therefore

\[\langle u_R\rangle =\frac12\mu_R\omega^2A_T^2.\]

The power fluxes are given by

\[P_L=Tk_L\omega \left( A_I^2\sin^2\phi_I -A_R^2\sin^2\phi_R \right),\]

and

\[P_R=Tk_R\omega A_T^2\sin^2\phi_T.\]

At the junction, the squared sine factors coincide because

\[\sin^2(-\omega t)=\sin^2(\omega t).\]

The amplitude relations also give

\[k_L(A_I^2-A_R^2)=k_RA_T^2.\]

Consequently,

\[P_L(0,t) =Tk_L\omega(A_I^2-A_R^2)\sin^2\omega t,\]

while

\[P_R(0,t) =Tk_R\omega A_T^2\sin^2\omega t.\]

Thus,

\[P_L(0,t)=P_R(0,t).\]

The reflected and transmitted fractions of the incident power are

\[\begin{aligned} R &=\frac{\langle P_{\mathrm{ref}}\rangle} {\langle P_I\rangle}\\ &=\left( \frac{\sqrt{\mu_L}-\sqrt{\mu_R}} {\sqrt{\mu_L}+\sqrt{\mu_R}} \right)^2, \end{aligned}\]

and

\[\begin{aligned} \mathcal{T} &=\frac{\langle P_T\rangle}{\langle P_I\rangle}\\ &=\frac{4\sqrt{\mu_L\mu_R}} {\left(\sqrt{\mu_L}+\sqrt{\mu_R}\right)^2}. \end{aligned}\]

In particular,

\[R+\mathcal{T}=1.\]

This confirms that, for an ideal junction without energy dissipation, the incident energy is divided between reflection and transmission.
