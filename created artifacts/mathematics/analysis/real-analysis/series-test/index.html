Abel and Dirichlet tests for series

A rudimentary exploration of some of the basic tests of convergence: Abel and Dirichlet

§1Introduction

Before begining, lets see what our intuition says. Consider the series:

\[\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_n = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n^2} = \frac{1}{1^2}+\frac{1}{2^2}+\frac{1}{3^2}+\cdots\]

and consider the sequence:

\[b_n = \frac{1}{n}\]

What do you think will the behaviour of the series be:

\[\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_n b_n = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n^3}\]

This in fact is a well known sequence, and it is well known to be convergent. In general if you have a converget series:

\[\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_n\]

and we multipy its terms by another sequence \((b_n)\). Your intuition would obviosuly say that the series:

\[\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_n b_n \]

is convergent.

An useful case is when \((b_n)\) is monotone and bounded.

Lemma 1.1 — Summation by Parts

Let

\[A_n=\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_k.\]

Then, for \(m\le n\),

\[\sum_{k=m}^{n}a_kb_k = A_nb_n-A_{m-1}b_m + \sum_{k=m}^{n-1}A_k(b_k-b_{k+1}).\]
Proof

Since

\[a_k=A_k-A_{k-1},\]

we have

\[\sum_{k=m}^{n}a_kb_k = \sum_{k=m}^{n}(A_k-A_{k-1})b_k.\]

Expanding,

\[= A_mb_m+A_{m+1}b_{m+1}+\cdots+A_nb_n - A_{m-1}b_m-A_mb_{m+1}-\cdots-A_{n-1}b_n.\]

Grouping the terms gives

\[\sum_{k=m}^{n}a_kb_k = A_nb_n-A_{m-1}b_m + A_m(b_m-b_{m+1}) +\cdots+ A_{n-1}(b_{n-1}-b_n).\]

Hence,

\[\sum_{k=m}^{n}a_kb_k = A_nb_n-A_{m-1}b_m + \sum_{k=m}^{n-1}A_k(b_k-b_{k+1}).\]
□

The summation by parts formula is just the discrete product rule written and summed. The terms \(A_kb_k\) cancel successively, leaving only the end terms and the changes \(b_k-b_{k+1}\).

Theorem 1.2 — Abel's Test

Suppose that

\[\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_n\]

converges and that \((b_n)\) is monotone and bounded. Then

\[\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_nb_n\]

converges.

Proof

Let

\[A_n=\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_k.\]

Since \(\sum a_n\) converges, \(A_n\to A\) for some \(A\in\mathbb R\). Also, since \((b_n)\) is monotone and bounded,

\[b_n\to b\]

for some \(b\in\mathbb R\).

We show that the partial sums of \(\sum a_nb_n\) form a Cauchy sequence.

Let \(\epsilon>0\). Since \(A_n\to A\), choose \(N\) such that

\[n\ge N\implies |A_n-A|<\epsilon.\]

Since \(b_n\) is monotone and converges, its total variation is bounded. Using summation by parts, for \(n>m\ge N\),

\[\sum_{k=m}^{n}a_kb_k = A_nb_n-A_{m-1}b_m + \sum_{k=m}^{n-1}A_k(b_k-b_{k+1}).\]

Subtracting \(A\) from each \(A_k\), the terms involving \(A\) cancel, because

\[Ab_m-Ab_n+\sum_{k=m}^{n-1}A(b_k-b_{k+1})=0.\]

Thus

\[\sum_{k=m}^{n}a_kb_k = (A_n-A)b_n-(A_{m-1}-A)b_m + \sum_{k=m}^{n-1}(A_k-A)(b_k-b_{k+1}).\]

Since \((b_n)\) is bounded, say \(|b_n|\le M\), and \(b_n\) is monotone,

\[\sum_{k=m}^{n-1}|b_k-b_{k+1}| \le 2M.\]

Therefore,

\[\left|\sum_{k=m}^{n}a_kb_k\right| \le 2M\epsilon+2M\epsilon = 4M\epsilon.\]

Hence the partial sums are Cauchy, and therefore

\[\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_nb_n\]

converges.

□
