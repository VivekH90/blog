Waves and Energy considerations in \(1\)D systems

Here we consider the basic energy transfer phenomenon in simple \(1\)D strings

Vivek Kalita ·17 October 2026
Classical MechanicsWave MechanicsEnergy ConservationFourier Analysis
Contents

§1Introduction

Let's start with a very basic question.

Consider a string, vibrating transversely. So any small element on the string would be executing a vertical oscillatory motion; is the net energy of the particle conserved?

Anyone's first view of this would be that the energy should be conserved; since it is executing simple harmonic motion. But as it turns out, it is not the case.

§2Energy transfer

Let's consider a discrete model of the string.

The force on the \(n\)-th segment is given by the equation:

\[F_n = \frac{T}{a}(y_{n+1}-y_n) - \frac{T}{a}(y_n-y_{n-1}).\]

where \(a\) is the seperation between the masses.

Define:

\[q_n = y_{n+1}-y_n, \qquad q_{n-1} = y_n-y_{n-1}.\]

Then for the kinetic energy of the \(n\)-th mass, which is given as:

\[K_n = \frac{1}{2}m\dot{y}_n^{\,2}.\]

we would have:

\[\frac{dK_n}{dt} = m\dot{y}_n\ddot{y}_n = \frac{T}{a}\dot{y}_n(q_n-q_{n-1}).\]

Likewise for the potential we would have:

\[U_n = \frac{T}{4a}\left(q_{n-1}^2+q_n^2\right) \qquad \text{and} \qquad \frac{dU_n}{dt} = \frac{T}{2a} \left(q_{n-1}\dot{q}_{n-1}+q_n\dot{q}_n\right).\]

So that for the net energy we would have:

\[\frac{dE_n}{dt}= \frac{dK_n}{dt}+\frac{dU_n}{dt}\\[6pt]= \frac{T}{2a}\left[ q_n(\dot{y}_{n+1}+\dot{y}_n) -q_{n-1}(\dot{y}_n+\dot{y}_{n-1}) \right]\]

In general this term is not zero:

\[\frac{dE_n}{dt}\neq 0.\]

§2.1Continuum limit and the energy flux

In general we can define:

\[P_{n+\frac12} = -\frac{T}{2a}q_n(\dot{y}_{n+1}+\dot{y}_n) \qquad \text{and} \qquad P_{n-\frac12} = -\frac{T}{2a}q_{n-1}(\dot{y}_n+\dot{y}_{n-1})\]

so that we have an energy transfer equation given as:

\[\frac{dE_n}{dt} = P_{n-\frac12}-P_{n+\frac12}.\]

Note that, although the energy is not conserved for each element. It however is conserved for the total string:

\[\sum_n \frac{dE_n}{dt}=0.\]

Taking the continuum limit:

\[x=na,\qquad y_n\to y,\qquad a\to 0.\]

we have:

\[y_{n+1}-y_n\sim a\frac{\partial y}{\partial x}, \qquad \dot{y}_{n+1}+\dot{y}_n \sim 2\frac{\partial y}{\partial t}.\]

so that:

\[P_{n+\frac12} \sim -\frac{T}{2a} \left(a\frac{\partial y}{\partial x}\right) \left(2\frac{\partial y}{\partial t}\right).\]

so that:

\[P(x,t)=-T\frac{\partial y}{\partial x}\frac{\partial y}{\partial t}.\]

and our continuity equation becomes:

\[\frac{\partial u}{\partial t} +\frac{\partial P}{\partial x}=0.\]

§2.2Power transfer in simple sinusoidal wave

For a right moving sinusoidal wave we have:

\[y(x,t)=A\cos(kx-\omega t), \qquad \omega=vk.\]

The instantaneous power therefore is,

\[P(x,t)=TA^2k\omega\sin^2(kx-\omega t).\]

and the time averaged power is:

\[\langle P\rangle=\frac{1}{2}TA^2k\omega=\frac{1}{2}\mu v\omega^2A^2.\]

§2.3Superposition and the Fourier Spectrum

Consider a superposition of two wavestwo waves:

\[y=A_1\cos\theta_1+A_2\cos\theta_2 \qquad \text{where} \qquad \theta_j=k_jx-\omega_jt.\]

It is easy to verify that the average power is the simple sum of the individual average powers:

\[\langle P\rangle =\frac{1}{2}TA_1^2k_1\omega_1 +\frac{1}{2}TA_2^2k_2\omega_2.\]

So that, in general for discrete fourier modes:

\[y=\sum_j A_j\cos\theta_j \qquad \text{we have} \qquad \langle P\rangle =\frac{1}{2}\sum_j TA_j^2k_j\omega_j.\]

§2.4A complete (continous) fourier spectrum

For a right moving wave, the complete Fourier spectrum is given by:

\[y(x,t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}} \int_{-\infty}^{+\infty} A(k)e^{ik(x-vt)}\,dk.\]

Since, we also have:

\[y_t=-vy_x.\]

The instantaneous power is therefore:

\[P(x,t)=Tv\left[y_x(x,t)\right]^2 =\frac{Tv}{2\pi} \left| \int_{-\infty}^{+\infty} ikA(k)e^{ik(x-vt)}\,dk \right|^2.\]

Parseval's theorem thus gives the tota Energy:

\[E=T\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}k^2|A(k)|^2\,dk.\]

§3Energy Transfer between strings

Consider two strings joined at \(x = 0\).

Let the left string have density \(\mu_L\) and the right one have density \(\mu_R\). The strings would have the same tension (to balance the horizontal force at \(x = 0\)).

The boundary conditions are:

\[y_L(0,t)=y_R(0,t) \qquad \text{and} \qquad \frac{\partial y_L(0,t)}{\partial x} = \frac{\partial y_R(0,t)}{\partial x}\]
